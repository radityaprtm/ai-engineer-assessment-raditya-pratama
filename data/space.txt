Apollo 11
Apollo 11 was the first crewed mission to land humans on the Moon. The mission was launched by NASA on July 16, 1969, using a Saturn V rocket. Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin landed the lunar module Eagle on the Moon on July 20, 1969, while Michael Collins remained in lunar orbit. Armstrong became the first person to walk on the Moon.

Voyager 1
Voyager 1 is a NASA space probe launched on September 5, 1977. Its original mission included flybys of Jupiter and Saturn. Voyager 1 later continued toward the outer edge of the Solar System and became the first human-made spacecraft to enter interstellar space. It carries the Voyager Golden Record, which contains sounds and images representing life and culture on Earth.

Hubble Space Telescope
The Hubble Space Telescope was launched in 1990 aboard the Space Shuttle Discovery. It orbits Earth above much of the atmosphere, allowing it to capture clearer astronomical images than many ground-based telescopes. Hubble has contributed to research on galaxies, stars, nebulae, black holes, and the expansion of the universe.

James Webb Space Telescope
The James Webb Space Telescope was launched on December 25, 2021. It primarily observes infrared light and operates near the Sun-Earth L2 point, about 1.5 million kilometers from Earth. Its scientific goals include studying the formation of early galaxies, the birth of stars and planetary systems, and the atmospheres of exoplanets.

International Space Station
The International Space Station, or ISS, is a crewed research laboratory in low Earth orbit. Construction began in 1998 through cooperation between multiple space agencies, including NASA, Roscosmos, ESA, JAXA, and CSA. Astronauts aboard the ISS conduct experiments in areas such as biology, physics, human health, and materials science.

Perseverance Rover
NASA's Perseverance rover landed in Jezero Crater on Mars on February 18, 2021. Its mission includes studying Martian geology, searching for signs of ancient microbial life, and collecting rock and soil samples for possible future return to Earth. Perseverance also carried the Ingenuity helicopter, which demonstrated powered flight on another planet.

New Horizons
New Horizons is a NASA spacecraft launched in 2006 to study Pluto and the outer Solar System. It performed the first close flyby of Pluto in July 2015, providing detailed images and scientific measurements of Pluto and its moons. The spacecraft later flew past the Kuiper Belt object Arrokoth in 2019.

Mars Curiosity Rover
NASA's Curiosity rover landed inside Gale Crater on Mars in August 2012. Its main goal is to investigate whether Mars once had environmental conditions suitable for microbial life. Curiosity studies Martian rocks, soil, atmosphere, and climate using scientific instruments carried aboard the rover.
